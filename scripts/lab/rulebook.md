# How this project weighs evidence about an EGX company

These are the evaluation rules the re-rank layer is given when "the rule book"
is one of the things it reads. They are condensed from the project's research
playbook. They describe how to weigh evidence, not what to buy: a high weight
means a company deserves a closer look, never that it should be owned.

## 1. The filing comes first, and its date matters

- Prefer primary sources: an exchange disclosure or its attachment, then a
  regulator's announcement, then the company's own release, then reputable
  financial press. Social media is rumour until a filing confirms it.
- Separate the date an event happened from the date it was published, and
  both from the first session the price or volume reacted.
- A catalyst that was public before the move is evidence. One that appeared
  during the move is weaker. One reported only afterwards explains nothing.

## 2. Materiality is relative to the company

An announcement matters more when:

- a transaction or asset is worth more than about 10% of the market value;
- a new contract is worth more than about 20% of annual revenue;
- quarterly profit growth exceeds about 50% and comes from operations rather
  than a one-off gain;
- a shareholder crosses 5%, or adds to an already material stake;
- a capital action has a known near-term date (record date, subscription
  close, allocation, listing);
- the event changes debt, cash generation, capacity, ownership or future
  earnings.

Always check the denominator. A spectacular percentage of a tiny base is not
a spectacular event.

## 3. Price and volume confirm; they do not replace evidence

- Measure volume against the company's own median of the previous 20
  sessions. Around three times normal is a meaningful change in activity.
- Early confirmation looks like unusual volume while the price has moved
  modestly, closing near the session's high, breaking from a base.
- A large move on tiny traded value is fragile.
- Two or more completed limit-up sessions usually mean the discovery is late.
- A price already up more than about 20% in five sessions has probably
  captured most of what the evidence justified.

## 4. Warnings that outweigh a good story

- The company says it knows of nothing explaining the move.
- Severe dilution, a stretched valuation, thin liquidity or no practical way
  to exit.
- A low-float squeeze with no filing behind it: detectable, not predictable.
- Successive limit sessions reflect the exchange's price limits and scarce
  supply, not an equal rise in what the business is worth.

## 5. Three different situations, never merged

1. A confirmed setup: a specific, material, dated filing, confirmed by
   activity, with enough liquidity to enter and leave.
2. A watch: an event or a pattern is present but part of the evidence is
   missing.
3. A tape alert: unusual price or volume with no verified evidence behind it.
   This is a question, not an answer.

## 6. What a model forecast is worth here

- A forecast from another model is one input. It awards no evidence of its
  own and never overrides a missing filing or a warning above.
- Missing data is missing. It is not a zero, and not a reason for optimism.
- Momentum on its own is not a reason to rank a company highly.
