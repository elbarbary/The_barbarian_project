Case 09 Published 3 September · corrected 5 September 2026

EGX / AMOC / ALEXANDRIA MINERAL OILS COMPANY

The company said it
in June.

On 29 June AMOC told the exchange, in writing, that it expected profit to roughly double. The stock rose 1.2% on a quarter of normal volume. A month later the results said the same thing and 44 million shares changed hands. EGP 7.52 on the day nobody read it. EGP 13.50 now. This is also the first partial score in the series — five pillars evidenced, one dark — and it is published as exactly that.

00 / Start here

AMOC in
normal English.

A state-linked mineral-oils and base-oils producer in Alexandria whose half-year profit more than doubled, whose accounts carry two independent sign-offs, and whose share price had already moved by the time most people noticed.

  1. 01

    It told you in June.

    On 29 June the board revised its budget and raised the profit expectation from EGP 1.024bn to EGP 2.099bn. That was a forecast, not a result — but it was public, on the exchange, in writing.

    +105% expected
  2. 02

    Nobody read it.

    The stock rose 1.21% that day on 2,997,776 shares — 0.4× normal volume. Close: EGP 7.52.

    +1% · quarter volume
  3. 03

    Then it happened.

    Reviewed H1 profit: EGP 1,920,706,543 against EGP 910,309,920 — +111%. The second quarter alone went from EGP 333.5m to EGP 1,166.6m.

    Q2 tripled
  4. 04

    Profit ran three times faster than sales.

    Preliminary revenue rose about 35%. Profit rose 111%. That gap is the most interesting fact in this file, and nothing in the filings explains it. It is not attributed to margins here, because the income-statement notes have not been read.

    Unexplained
  5. 05

    Two auditors signed. The cash is still unseen.

    A Crowe Global member firm audited it and the state auditor approved it — a clean review from the same body that filed findings against Delta Sugar. But cash conversion is untested and the balance sheet is unread, so this is Loose Change, not Cash.

    Two signatures, no cash test

Three numbers that must never be blended. The 29 June figure is a budget — an expectation, not a result. The 30 July figure is preliminary and unaudited. The 16 August figure is reviewed — a limited review, which is not an annual audit. Each is quoted here only as what it is.

The distance between reading and noticing. 29 June close EGP 7.52; 2 September close EGP 13.50: +79.5%. The information that produced that move was free, public and specific on the first of those dates.

The trailing twelve months is the honest unit, and it is less flattering than the half. July 2025 to June 2026: EGP 2,577,135,254 — 6.77× earnings at EGP 13.50, a 14.8% earnings yield. Doubling H1 gives 4.54× and 22%, but that is a run-rate scenario resting on a quarter that tripled for a reason nothing in the filings identifies. Neither number is presented as established cheapness, and an earnings yield is not a deposit rate.

The fiscal year changed. AMOC ran a July–June year and filed a transitional July–December 2025 period (EGP 656,428,711 against EGP 641,640,411, +2.3%) before moving to calendar reporting. There is no ordinary "FY2025", and the H1 comparative is the second half of the old year.

A real lead, held as a lead. On 27 October 2025 AMOC announced an agreement to process its diesel at MIDOR, targeting roughly 350,000 tonnes a year of higher-quality diesel. It is the most plausible honest candidate for what changed in Q2. It is not established as the cause.

03 / What I got wrong

I said August.
It was July.

The 3 September report flagged that the largest move came on 13 August, the board-meeting day, "three days before the numbers were published." That framing was wrong, and the rules of this series require it to stay visible rather than be quietly fixed.

  1. 01

    The numbers were public on 30 July.

    Disclosure 292184 carried preliminary H1 indicators — consolidated profit EGP 1.903bn against 910.3m. The reviewed figure two weeks later was EGP 1.921bn. Nothing material changed between the two.

    Public, 30 Jul
  2. 02

    And the tape reacted that day — harder than in August.

    +6.96% on 44,465,398 shares, 6.0× median volume — the heaviest session in the window. The 13 August session was +7.85% on 3.3×. The market moved when the information appeared, not before it.

    6.0× on the news
  3. 03

    The June budget had already said it.

    The doubling was signalled on 29 June. What happened in August was confirmation of a fact that had been disclosed twice already.

    Signalled in June

Why this matters beyond one entry. This series ran the same leak-signature test on DGTZ and found the market genuinely did not know. It is the right test — but it only works if the date of first disclosure is right. Here it was not, and the corrected timeline is a better story than the wrong one: it says the information was free and nobody read it. No allegation was made in the original, and none is made now.

What two signatures do not tell you. They tell you the profit is properly stated. They do not tell you it turned into cash, or that the quarter which tripled will repeat. That is why earnings quality is scored zero and not positive.

Governance +3. Dual audit, clean state review, audit-and-governance committee, TCFD/ESG, bilingual reporting, every filing inside the deadline. Deducted for the combined chairman/managing-director role and the same-day Article 30 correction.

Dividend, and a trap. Coupon 22, EGP 0.40 in two EGP 0.20 instalments — paid 22 April 2026, and due 15 October 2026. End of right was 19 April 2026. A buyer today does not receive the October instalment.

What would move it up: the H1 statements showing operating cash flow tracking the EGP 1.92bn profit — one secondary report puts it near EGP 1.41bn but mislabels the period, so it is a lead, not a figure — and an identified, repeatable reason for the Q2 step-change. Those two alone take +16 to +21–26 with the balance sheet unchanged. What would move it down: a Q2 driven by a one-off, or a balance sheet the half-year profit is flattering.
