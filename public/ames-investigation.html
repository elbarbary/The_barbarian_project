EGX / AMES / ALEXANDRIA NEW MEDICAL CENTER

One hospital.
EGP 29 billion.

A 38-year-old hospital in Smouha, Alexandria. 268 beds. It earned EGP 55 million last year. Four years ago four buyers competed for it in a real auction and the highest number any of them reached was EGP 822 million. The market now says EGP 29.36 billion. This is every session that got it there — and the three things that fooled almost everybody along the way.

01 / The benchmark

Five people put a number
on this company.

Not analysts. Buyers, with money, in a contested sale. This is the strongest valuation evidence that exists for any company — better than a model, and far better than a quoted price in a 4% float.

Note which way the disagreement ran. The board rejected EGP 49 as too low — it "did not reflect the true value of the company" and sat below the six-month average close. Their own valuer said EGP 52.80. So the company's own position in 2022 was that it was worth more than EGP 822m. Marking it on Cleopatra Hospitals' multiples today gives EGP 3.94–6.69 a share. Two unrelated methods, one answer: roughly EGP 3–7, against EGP 120.03.

02 / The trigger

April wasn't news.
It was a deadline.

For three months AMES traded on nothing — tens of thousands of shares a day, drifting down. Then a regulatory timetable landed, and the tape tracked it line for line.

03 / The one-pound share

It looked like a gift.
It wasn't one.

This is the part almost every write-up got wrong, including an earlier draft of this one.

The offer, in plain language, was a supermarket promo: you own one share worth about EGP 52, and you get to buy a second one for EGP 1. Precisely, 0.9632 new shares at par for every old share held — 120,000,000 new shares in total.

It reads like free money. It isn't, and the reason matters more than the story.

A rights issue is pro-rata. Everyone is offered new shares in exact proportion to what they already own. Nobody's slice of the company changes. It is the same pizza cut into more slices — which is precisely what a rights issue is for: it is the mechanism that stops a majority holder issuing cheap stock to itself and diluting everyone else.

So the EGP 1 price is not a discount to anyone. It looks like one next to EGP 52, but a share sold cheap to everybody, in proportion is just a re-slicing. And the market prices that immediately: on 28 April the share fell by almost exactly the value of what had detached from it.

And nobody was short-changed. The float received 4,691,532 new shares against an entitlement of 4,691,532 — exact to the share. That is why the ownership split is 96.088% / 3.90961% both before and after. Net of both subscription stages, every side took up precisely what it was owed.

This also disposes of a claim that circulated widely — including in an earlier version of this file — that small shareholders "left their rights on the table". 3,091,984 shares were reported uncovered, but that is a first-stage figure. Those shares were re-offered and allocated in a second stage, and the final register shows no shortfall for anyone. Whose entitlement went unsubscribed at stage one is not determinable from the published aggregates, and no inference should be drawn from it.

04 / 28 April

The chart said −38%.
It was a limit-up.

The single most misread session in the whole episode, and a lesson that transfers to any stock going through a corporate action.

  • 27 April closelast session carrying the right EGP 62.52
  • Theoretical ex-rights pricethe share alone, once the right detached EGP 32.34this is the honest starting point
  • 28 April closewhat actually printed EGP 38.80+19.98% vs theoretical — limit up

On a raw chart that session is a 37.9% crash. It was nothing of the kind. The coupon came off the box: the share stopped carrying the right to buy a second share for a pound, so the share by itself was naturally worth less — and the exchange's own theoretical price said EGP 32.34. It closed at EGP 38.80, which is the maximum daily gain the EGX permits.

A holder lost nothing. Your one share was worth less, and you now held a right worth roughly the difference. The people who panicked sold into the stock's best possible session.

The transferable lesson: before you read a red candle as a collapse, check whether a corporate action detached that day. Splits, bonus issues, rights and dividends all do this, and most charting apps do not tell you.

It rose 43% before
the news that explains it.

The rally began on 29 June and the number that supposedly caused it was published on 7 July — by which point the stock had already risen 43.3%. And on the day the 476.93× was actually disclosed, it fell 1.19%.

There is a second problem with the easy explanation. Anyone buying the ordinary share on 29 June could not receive the one-pound shares — eligibility closed on 27 April, two months earlier. Whatever those buyers were buying, it was not the discount.

The company was asked, and said it did not know. On 14 July AMES told the exchange it had no information explaining the movement. The disclosure record supports that: there is no company filing between 17 June and 7 July.

What the 476.93× actually measured. Of 120,000,000 shares, 116,908,016 were taken up, leaving 3,091,984 to be re-offered — EGP 3.09m of stock at par. Those attracted roughly EGP 1.475 billion of orders, so applicants received about 0.21% of what they asked for. It is a ratio produced by a very small denominator, not a referendum on the hospital. The company raised EGP 120m either way.

No allegation of manipulation, coordination or leaked information is made here, and none is supported by the record. An unexplained move is a finding, not an accusation.

06 / Tradability

1.99% of this company
can actually be traded.

The share count doubled. The number of shares anyone can buy did not change by one.

  • Shares in issueafter the capital increase 244,581,600
  • Held by two shareholdersLimeVest 61.358% + Tawasol 34.733% 235,013,56896.088%
  • Free float on papercompany filing, 21 July 2026 9,562,1873.90961%
  • Actually admitted to tradingthe 120m new shares were not listed as at 30 July 4,870,6551.99% of the company

A thin float does not push a price up. It amplifies whatever order flow arrives, in both directions — which is exactly what the record shows. On 30 June, EGP 162m of trading moved the stock +19.45%. On 16 July, EGP 127m moved it −16.20%. Same float, same mechanism, opposite sign.

The cleanest illustration is 28 April: EGP 8.6m of trading added EGP 805m to the quoted value of the listed shares — a ratio of 94 to 1. Across April as a whole, EGP 386m of trading created EGP 4.07bn of quoted market cap.

Which means the quoted price is not a price you can transact at scale. Selling 1% of the majority holders' new shares would be 24% of the entire tradable float; 5% would be 118% of it. The EGP 28.2bn their stake is quoted at is a quotation, not a sum of money.

An open question. EGX listing rules require a maintained free float of 10% of total shares, or 0.0125% of total exchange free-float capital subject to a floor of 5% of the company's shares. AMES sits at 3.91% on paper and 1.99% in practice. What permits that — grandfathering, a remedy period, or a different measurement basis — I have not established, and I am not going to guess at it.

Vendor caution: data platforms report AMES's free float as 27.22%. The company's own 21 July 2026 filing says 3.90961% — a seven-fold difference. Take the float from a filing, never from a vendor.

08 / Method

Where I got this wrong
before I got it right.

Rule six of the series is publish the misses. That includes my own.

The corporate-action trap that nearly ruined this analysis. The daily price series from at least one major data vendor is rights-adjusted for every bar before the 28 April ex-date, and raw from 28 April onward. Pre-ex prices are divided by 1.93341 and volumes multiplied by it.

Used naively it produces three errors at once. April's move reads as +141% when the real move into the record date was +42.5% — the rest is the ex-rights drop. Float turnover reads as 206% instead of 130%. And applying the rights benefit on top of an already-adjusted price double-counts it, turning a true 5.09× into a false 9.64×.

I fell into it, and my test for it was invalid. I checked the series against two known quotes — the 3 May high of EGP 65.87 and the 14 July close of EGP 120.79 — both matched exactly, and I concluded the data was raw. Both of those dates fall after the ex-date, where the two hypotheses are identical. A post-ex-date check cannot detect a pre-ex-date adjustment.

The valid test is a contemporaneous press quote from before the ex-date. Al Mal's reported closes — EGP 46.97 on 10 March, EGP 56.76 on 21 April, EGP 64.66 on 26 April — are each exactly 1.93341× the vendor series. The ex-rights identity corroborates it: cum-rights EGP 62.52 → theoretical EGP 32.34 → actual EGP 38.80 = +19.98%, matching the session change as the exchange reported it.

Two other corrections made in public: an earlier version of this case adjusted the 2022 control price for the 8:1 par split but not for the rights issue, understating the re-rating as 19.6× instead of 33.2×. And an earlier version claimed small shareholders had abandoned rights worth thirty times their cost — that was an inference from a first-stage "uncovered" figure, and the final register refutes it. Both are corrected above.

Returns note: a holder who bought on 1 April and subscribed to the increase is at 5.09×. A holder who bought on 1 April and simply held, without subscribing, is at 2.65×. The two are not interchangeable and the difference is the whole point of section 03.
