00 / The premise

The whole exchange is
smaller than my chemistry syllabus.

That's a joke. It might also be true — and that's exactly the point. The EGX is small enough that one person can genuinely read all of it. So I'm going to.

The problem

Most EGX coverage is a chart with an opinion attached.

A price line, an arrow, a target. Nobody shows you the balance sheet, the free float, or whether last year's profit was a one-off asset sale dressed up as a business.

The method

Same six questions, every company, every time.

Six pillars scored −10 to +10. Every figure carries a source and a date. The verdict is about the stock at today's price — not about whether the people running it are any good.

The honest bit

A great company can be Trash.

And a boring one can be Cash. Price is half the verdict. If I score something Trash and it triples, that entry stays on the board with its original date attached.

01 / The scoring

Six pillars.
−10 to +10 each.

Total range −60 to +60. No pillar is scored from a chart, a tip or a rumour — only from filings, financial statements and exchange data.

01

Valuation

What you pay versus what you get. Earnings multiples, price against look-through book value, and market cap against what the assets are realistically worth.

02

Earnings quality

Is the profit real and repeatable? One-off disposals, FX gains and revaluations get stripped out. Profit recognised but not collected is penalised.

03

Growth

Three-year revenue and profit trend, plus the direction of the latest quarter. A collapsing quarter overrides a good three-year record.

04

Balance sheet

Can it survive a bad year? Leverage, negative equity anywhere in the group, rescheduled debt, and weak subsidiaries hidden outside the headline numbers.

05

Tradability

The pillar everyone ignores. Free float, median daily traded value, and days-to-exit. If you can't sell it, the quoted price isn't a price.

06

Governance

Regulator findings, restatements, related-party density, circular holdings, filing timeliness — and, as a positive, whether the company answers the exchange when asked.

🗑️ TRASH

AMES Alexandria New Medical Center

−17of −60 … +60
  • ⚠️ Thin float
  • 🔒 Can't exit
  • 🚩 Governance

One 38-year-old hospital in Smouha. Carrying more market value than the entire Cleopatra group.

  • Valuation−10
  • Earnings quality−5
  • Growth+3
  • Balance sheet+5
  • Tradability−8
  • Governance−2
−100+10

The case: at EGP 120.03 the stock trades on 520.9× trailing earnings and 61.1× sales, for a single hospital earning EGP 56m. It carries 122% of Cleopatra Hospitals' market cap on 6.6% of its revenue, 6.8% of its owner profit and 4.0% of its EBITDA — one facility against fourteen. The balance sheet is the one genuine positive: net cash, no debt.

Note which way the disagreement ran: the board rejected EGP 49 as too low — it "did not reflect the true value of the company" and sat below the six-month average close. Their own valuer said EGP 52.80. The company's own position in 2022 was that it was worth more than EGP 822m. Marking it on Cleopatra's multiples today gives EGP 3.94–6.69 a share. Two unrelated methods, one answer: roughly EGP 3–7.

The capital increase, decoded: EGP 120m raised at EGP 1 par against a ~EGP 62 market price — a ~98% discount. The celebrated 476.93× oversubscription was ~EGP 1.47bn of cash chasing 3,091,984 shares worth EGP 3.09m: a 0.21% allocation. That ratio measures a tiny denominator, not demand for the business.

The float that never grew: the share count doubled, but free float went 3.91% → 3.90961%. Vendors report 27.22% — the company's own 21 July filing says otherwise, and half the new shares had not even been listed. Two holders own 96.088%.

What would flip it: roughly 18× the current profit, or a price back near EGP 3–7. Note AMES told the exchange it knew of no reason for the move on 14 July — five days before BIOC did the same — and the stock broke two sessions later, −16.20%.

Where this hospital came from — context, not an accusation. It opened in 1988. In 2009 it was bought in his personal capacity by BR Shetty, who separately founded the UAE healthcare group NMC Health, and who announced a AED 180m plan to take it from 150 beds to 400. NMC collapsed in 2020 after understating its debts by as much as USD 4bn — the UK's FCA censured it for misleading the market in November 2023. Shetty left the AMES board in April 2020; AMES said at the time that it was unaffected, and nothing in this research connects AMES itself to NMC's accounting problems. ADCB — NMC's largest creditor, with USD 981m of exposure — took Shetty's 51.54% of AMES in December 2020 for AED 64m (~USD 17.4m) to settle his debts, then put it up for sale. That is the auction above. A USD 5.4bn civil fraud claim brought against Shetty by NMC's administrators went to trial at ADGM Courts in March 2026; he denies all culpability, says he is himself the victim of a fraud, and no court has made a finding against him.

The case: at EGP 194 the stock trades on roughly 102× trailing earnings while Q1 2026 profit fell 92% and sales fell 18%. But the killer is the float: 2.91% — 2,434,272 shares. In one session 45.8% of the entire float changed hands, and across ten sessions the float turned over 3.54 times. Governance actually scores positive — the company answered the exchange promptly and its ownership filings are clean.

Structural note: BIOC was added to EGX70 EWI and EGX100 EWI in the review announced 27 January 2026. Index inclusion forces buying into an almost non-existent float — a mechanical driver, not a company event.

What would flip it: a real free float. At 2.91%, the quoted price is not a price you can transact at scale in either direction.

CIRA Cairo for Investment and Real Estate Development

The best business this series has examined. And still a no at EGP 36.80.

  • Valuation−3
  • Earnings quality+5
  • Growth+6
  • Balance sheet−1
  • Tradability−4
  • Governance−4
−100+10

Every sceptical attack failed. The growth is not tuition inflation: 9M revenue +35.5% splits into students +19.3% × revenue per student +13.5% against ~12.7% CPI, so real pricing is about +0.5% and the growth is volume. Higher Education — 69% of revenue — raised fees 1.0% nominally, a 10.5% cut in real terms, while adding 10,100 students. The profit is clean: historical-cost accounts, one-offs 4.4% of PBT, and management's "normalised" figure is lower than statutory. Trade receivables fell 3.1% while revenue rose 35%, against deferred revenue of 9× the receivable — tuition is collected before it is taught.

So what kills it is the price. CIRA earns a 4.69% earnings yield at EGP 36.80 while its own audited notes show its bank deposits earning 18% against a 19% policy rate — a ~14-point negative spread to the risk-free rate, at the 52-week high, after +78% in three months. Do not annualise the 9M EPS: Q4 runs June–August and is structurally loss-making, so the real forward multiple is 20–26×, not the 13× that naive annualisation manufactures.

And the engine is nearly full: higher-ed seat utilisation 64% → 88% with faculties up 0%, K-12 at 93.6%, and the replacement campus slipped about two years. Plus a live covenant breach with KFH and QNB — EGP 1,994.4m reclassified as current, disclosed only in the Arabic filings.

What would flip it — price alone. At EGP 20–22 the total clears into Loose Change with nothing else changing.

04 / The rules I hold myself to

So you can
check my work.

  1. Every number cites a source and an as-of date. No figure goes in without one.
  2. Price and fundamentals are dated together. Never today's market cap against an eighteen-month-old book value without saying so.
  3. The corporate-action sanitizer runs first. A bonus issue or split can manufacture a fake percentage. Share count is checked before and after.
  4. Score the stock, not the story. No verdict rests on momentum, a chart pattern, a tip channel or a rumour.
  5. Every entry names what would flip it. If I can't say that, I don't understand it well enough to publish.
  6. The misses stay up. When a Trash call triples or a Cash call sinks, the entry keeps its original date and score.
