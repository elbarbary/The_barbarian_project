# Cash or Trash — EGX scoring criteria

Series start: 29 July 2026
Scope: every listed company on the Egyptian Exchange (~224 names)
Author: The Barbarian Project · thebarbarianproject.com

> **I am not telling anyone to invest in anything.** This is a study of the EGX, not advice.
> The whole exchange is smaller than my STEM-school chemistry curriculum — that is a joke,
> but it might also be true, and that is exactly why one person can read all of it.

## Why this exists

Most EGX coverage is a price chart with an opinion attached. This series asks one question per
company and answers it the same way every time: **if you had to own this business, would you?**

The verdict is about the *stock at today's price*, not about whether the people running it are good
at their jobs. A fine company at an absurd price is Trash. A dull company at a cheap price can be Cash.

## The six pillars

Each pillar scores **−10 to +10**. Total range **−60 to +60**. Every score must cite a filing,
a financial statement or exchange data — never a chart pattern and never a tip.

### 1. Valuation — what you pay versus what you get
Trailing and forward P/E, price-to-book against *look-through* book value (not headline equity),
EV/EBITDA where computable, and market cap against the realistic value of the underlying assets.

| Score | Condition |
|---:|---|
| +8 to +10 | Cheap on earnings *that are real*, and cheap on assets |
| +1 to +7 | Reasonable for the quality of the business |
| −1 to −7 | Expensive; needs strong growth to justify |
| −8 to −10 | Price detached from any earnings or book anchor |

### 2. Earnings quality — is the profit real and repeatable?
The share of net profit that comes from one-offs (asset disposals, FX gains, revaluations),
cash conversion (free cash flow versus reported net income), and whether the revenue was
actually *collected* — check receivables and their ageing.

- Automatic **−8 or worse** if a majority of the headline profit is a single non-recurring item.
- Automatic **−5 or worse** if profit is recognised but most of the cash is still a receivable.
- Look at profit *attributable to owners*, not the consolidated headline, when minorities are large.

### 3. Growth — direction, not just level
Three-year revenue and net-profit trend, plus the direction of the most recent quarter.
A collapsing latest quarter overrides a good three-year record.

### 4. Balance sheet — can it survive a bad year?
Leverage, negative equity anywhere in the group, rescheduled or defaulted debt, going-concern
language, and whether a weak subsidiary is hidden outside the headline numbers.

### 5. Tradability — can you actually get out?
**The pillar retail screens ignore, and the one that hurts most.**
Free float percentage and share count, median daily traded value, and *days-to-exit*: how many
sessions it would take to sell a position at 20% of median daily volume. Also exposure to the
EGX ±20% daily price limit, which cuts both ways.

| Score | Condition |
|---:|---|
| +5 to +10 | Deep float, you can build and exit freely |
| −1 to −4 | Thin but workable |
| −5 to −10 | Float so small the quoted price is not a price you can transact at scale |

### 6. Governance & disclosure — does the filing record hold up?
Regulator findings (FRA / EGX listing committee), prior-period restatements, density of
related-party transactions, circular or cross-holdings, filing timeliness, and — a good sign —
whether the company answers the exchange promptly when asked.

## Verdict bands

| Total | Verdict | Meaning |
|---:|---|---|
| **+20 and above** | 💰 **CASH** | Worth researching further as an investment |
| **+5 to +19** | 🪙 **LOOSE CHANGE** | Something real here, but the price or the risk is in the way |
| **−29 to +4** | 🗑️ **TRASH** | Not an investment at this price |
| **−30 and below** | ☠️ **TOXIC** | Structural problems beyond valuation |

## Flags (badges, not scores)

- ⚠️ **THIN FLOAT** — free float under 10%
- 🔒 **CAN'T EXIT** — more than 20 sessions to unwind at 20% of median volume
- 📉 **ONE-OFF EARNINGS** — majority of profit non-recurring
- 🚩 **GOVERNANCE** — an open or unresolved regulator finding
- 🔄 **CIRCULAR OWNERSHIP** — the company owns part of its owner, or vice versa
- ❓ **NO DATA** — filings too incomplete to score honestly

## Rules of the series

1. **Every number cites a source and an as-of date.** No figure goes in without one.
2. **Price and fundamentals must be dated together.** Never compare today's market cap to
   an eighteen-month-old book value without saying so.
3. **Run the corporate-action sanitizer first.** A bonus issue, split or capital change can
   manufacture a fake percentage. Check the share count before and after.
4. **Score the stock, not the story.** No verdict is based on momentum, a chart pattern,
   a tip channel or a rumour.
5. **Say what would change the verdict.** Every entry names the fact that would flip it.
6. **Publish the misses.** When a Trash call goes up or a Cash call goes down, it stays on the
   leaderboard with the original date attached.

**Why the per-share column reads lower than the market-cap multiple.** Andersen restated is EGP 3.85, i.e. 31.2× —
not 35.7×. The gap is exactly the **EGP 120m of rights-issue cash**: the per-share restatement credits the company
with money it did not have in 2022, the market-cap comparison does not. Both are right; they answer different
questions. Check: 822.2m + 120m = 942.2m, and 942.2m ÷ 244,581,600 = EGP 3.852. **Quote one basis at a time** —
mixing them in a single sentence reads as an arithmetic error even though neither figure is wrong.

*Method:* each 2021/22 price is divided by 8 for the 2024 par split, then adjusted for the 2026 rights issue on a theoretical ex-rights basis — (124,581,600 × split-adjusted price + 120,000,000 × EGP 1) ÷ 244,581,600. **Correction (1 Aug 2026): an earlier version of this entry applied the split but not the rights issue, and so published 19.6× and 18.2×. The rights-adjusted figures above are the correct ones.**

Note the direction of the disagreement: the board **rejected** EGP 49 as *too low*, saying it "did not reflect the true value of the company" and was below the six-month average close. Their own valuer said EGP 52.80. So the company's position in 2022 was that it was worth **more** than EGP 822m. It is now priced at 36× that.

**Versus Cleopatra Hospitals (CLHO):** AMES carries **122% of CLHO's market cap** on **6.6% of its revenue, 6.8% of its owner profit and 4.0% of its EBITDA** — one facility against fourteen. Marked on CLHO's own multiples across today's 244,581,600 shares, AMES comes out at **EGP 3.94 (EBITDA) to EGP 6.69 (owner profit) a share.**

**Two independent methods, one answer.** The 2021 auction band is EGP 2.94–3.85. The peer band is EGP 3.94–6.69. They overlap. **A fair-value range of roughly EGP 3–7 a share is supported by two completely unrelated lines of evidence — a competitive auction and a listed peer — against EGP 120.03 today.**

**What would flip it:** roughly 18× the current profit, or a price back near EGP 3–7. Note the company denied knowing any reason for the move on **14 July — five days before BIOC did the same** — and the stock broke two sessions later, −16.20% on 16 July.

**Provenance (context, not an allegation).** The hospital opened in 1988 and was bought in 2009 *in his personal capacity* by BR Shetty, who separately founded NMC Health. NMC collapsed in 2020 after understating its debts by as much as USD 4bn (FCA censure, Nov 2023). Shetty left the AMES board in April 2020 and the company said at the time it was unaffected; **no source in this research connects AMES itself to NMC's accounting problems.** ADCB, NMC's largest creditor, took Shetty's 51.54% AMES stake in Dec 2020 for AED 64m (~USD 17.4m) in settlement of his debts, then auctioned it. A USD 5.4bn civil fraud claim against Shetty by NMC's administrators went to trial at ADGM Courts in March 2026; **he denies all culpability and says he is himself the victim of a fraud. No finding has been made against him.**

### A recurring lesson from the first six

The first three are thin-float names, and in every case the *vendor* float number was wrong or misleading.
TradingView reported AMES at **27.22%** free float; the company's own filing says **3.90961%** — off by
seven times. Always take the float from an Article 30 filing, never from a data vendor.

**"47% of the shares are frozen" is a financing pledge, not a mystery.** (added 4 Aug 2026)
MCDR custody data shows 275,357,825 CIRA shares — 47.24% of capital — classified as "frozen for other
various reasons", and I nearly carried that as a governance red flag. Note 14 of the 9M filing explains it:
**8,329,190 shares of Cairo for Educational Services are pledged**, and the loan terms require
*"رهن كامل أسهم شركة الأهلي سيرا للخدمات التعليمية لصالح البنك"* — a full pledge of the Al Ahly CIRA shares
to the lender. Pledged shares (مرهونة) show as frozen in MCDR reporting. This is ordinary security for bank
financing, disclosed in the ordinary place. **Standing rule: before treating a custody classification as a
scandal, read the loan note — "frozen" in MCDR usually means pledged, locked-up or otherwise encumbered,
not concealed.**

**How an EGX buyout is actually priced — and a correction I had to make.** (added 4 Aug 2026)
While drafting the CIRA video I claimed that if a controlling holder reached 90% it could delist, and that
minorities would be bought out on a **six-month average** — for CIRA, about EGP 23 against a EGP 36.80 market
price. **Both halves were wrong**, and the direction was wrong too:

- **90% is not a delisting trigger.** It is a *proration* mechanic: a bidder who undertakes to keep the company
  listed must offer for 100% less the 10% minimum free float, and if more than 90% accept, acceptances are
  prorated to preserve that 10%.
- **Voluntary delisting** requires a **75% majority at an extraordinary general meeting**, and a recent
  amendment adds the approval of a majority of *minority* shareholders, not merely those present.
- **The dissenting-shareholder price** is the **higher** of (a) the highest price traded in the preceding
  **month** or (b) the average closing price over the preceding **three months**. For CIRA on 3 Aug that is
  the higher of EGP 37.55 and EGP 28.39 = **EGP 37.55, about 102% of the market price.**

So the rule is a **floor that protects minorities, not a cap that traps them.** The standing lesson: before
building any argument on a takeover or delisting mechanic, read the rule — do not reason from a precedent.
The AMES six-month-average reference came from a *company's own complaint about an offer*, not from the
delisting rules, and I generalised it into something it never said.
*(Sourced from FRA/EGX listing-and-delisting rules as summarised by law-firm and press coverage, including the
2024 amendments. I have not read the rule text itself, so treat the mechanics as well-supported but not
primary-sourced.)*

**CIRA inverts the lesson without repealing it.** Its vendor float of 11.32% was *corroborated* by the Article 30 filing at 11.30%. The rule is not "vendors are always wrong" — it is "never rely on a vendor without checking". Checking is cheap; the AMES error was 7× and inverted the conclusion.

### Two method rules earned the hard way (added 1 Aug 2026)

**Adjust for *every* capital action, not just the obvious one.** Comparing a historical price to today's
requires unwinding the split *and* the rights issue. AMES's first published entry adjusted for the 8:1 split
but not the 120m-share rights issue, and understated the re-rating by about 40% (19.6× instead of 33.2×).
The theoretical ex-rights adjustment is
`(old shares × split-adjusted price + new shares × subscription price) ÷ total shares`.

**Then find out whether your vendor already did it — and test on the correct side of the ex-date.**
The AMES daily series is rights-adjusted for every bar *before* the 28 April 2026 ex-date and raw from
that date onward. Pre-ex prices are divided by 1.93341 and volumes multiplied by it. Used naively it
produces three errors at once: April's move reads +141% when the real move into the record date was
+42.5%; float turnover reads 206% instead of 130%; and applying the rights benefit on top of an
already-adjusted price double-counts it, turning a true 5.09× into a false 9.64×.
**A check against a post-ex-date quote cannot detect this** — I confirmed two such points (the 3 May
high and the 14 July close), concluded the series was raw, and was wrong. The only valid test is a
contemporaneous press quote from *before* the ex-date: Al Mal's reported closes of EGP 46.97 (10 Mar),
56.76 (21 Apr) and 64.66 (26 Apr) are each exactly 1.93341× the vendor series. Corroborate with the
ex-rights identity — cum-rights 62.52 → theoretical 32.34 → actual 38.80 = +19.98%, matching the
reported session change.

**A competitive auction is the best valuation evidence there is.** Better than my peer multiples, better than
a DCF, and far better than a quoted price in a 4% float — because it is several independent parties with real
money reaching the same number at arm's length. Whenever a name has been through a contested M&A process,
dig the bids out and restate them. It converts an opinion into a measurement.
