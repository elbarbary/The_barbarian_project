Questions & Answers for Everyday Investors

Everything an Egyptian Investor Needs to Know

Clear, jargon-free answers to how the system actually manages risk and trades on the Egyptian Exchange.

1. Why did selling 100% on every alert do worse in this test?

Selling 100% (like the S100 test model above) is the most common amateur mistake. In the real world, dumping everything causes three major problems:
• Heavy Friction: You pay double brokerage commissions and stamp tax every time you exit and re-enter (S100 generated an insane 841%/yr turnover!).
• Whipsaw: If an alert lasts only 3 days and the market bounces, you sold at the bottom.
• Missing Rebounds: The best single days on the EGX happen right after crashes. If you are 100% in cash, you miss the explosion.
In this backtest, a partial hedge (25% to 60%) kept part of the portfolio invested through recoveries. That is a finding about past data, not a recommendation.

2. What are Egyptian T-Bills (أذون خزانة) and why do we use them instead of cash?

Egyptian Treasury Bills are guaranteed debt securities issued by the Central Bank of Egypt. In Egypt, they offer high risk-free interest rates (often 15% to 25%+ per year).
When our strategy hedges, it doesn't hold dead cash sitting in a bank account losing to inflation. Instead, the hedged portion is parked in Egyptian T-bills, actively earning guaranteed daily compounding interest while waiting for stocks to become safe again.

3. Why look at Gold and Brent Oil if I only invest in Cairo stocks?

Egypt relies heavily on imported wheat and fuel. When global oil spikes, it drains Egypt's foreign currency reserves and inflates corporate costs. When global gold surges, Egyptian investors rush into gold as a currency hedge, draining liquidity from stocks.
If both Gold and Oil are flashing red globally, an Egyptian market dip is not a minor wobble — it's a structural contagion shock. The World Monitor uses this global radar to raise your defensive shield from 25% up to 60%.

4. What did the score read in the last research run?

5. What is the "Volatility Brake" and why not buy back immediately?

When an alert ends, amateur investors often rush back in immediately — only to get hit by a secondary crash (a "dead cat bounce").
The C-v4 Volatility Brake checks whether EGX 20-day volatility is expanding above 28%. If the market is still swinging wildly like a falling knife, the brake delays re-entry until the volatility subsides, saving investors from false bottoms.

6. Does this simulation take real broker fees and taxes into account?

Yes. Every simulation and curve on this page strictly subtracts 20 basis points (0.20%) one-way (40 bps round-trip) on every single transaction, and executes at the next day's official market opening price ($T+1\text{ Open}$). There is zero unrealistic "frictionless" data leakage.

te_prices = prices_all[eval_indices]

# 2. Risk-free rate setup
rf_raw_yield = np.array([float(tb_raw[d]) if d in tb_raw else 10.0 for d in eval_dates])

rf_daily_zero = np.zeros(N_eval, dtype=float)

# Simulation Engine Function
def simulate_weight_path(w_des, rf_series=rf_daily_gross, fee_rate=FEE):
    cur_w = 1.0
    p_rets = np.zeros(N_eval)
    turnover = 0.0
    for i in range(N_eval):
        des_w = w_des[i]
        tc = abs(des_w - cur_w) * fee_rate
        turnover += abs(des_w - cur_w)
        cur_w = des_w
        p_rets[i] = cur_w * te_ret[i] + (1.0 - cur_w) * rf_series[i] - tc
        
    cum = np.cumprod(1.0 + p_rets)
    cagr = (cum[-1] ** (1.0 / N_YEARS) - 1.0) * 100.0
    dd = (cum - np.maximum.accumulate(cum)) / np.maximum.accumulate(cum)
    max_dd = float(np.min(dd)) * 100.0
    vol = float(np.std(p_rets) * np.sqrt(250) * 100.0)
    mean_w = float(np.mean(w_des) * 100.0)
    ann_turnover = float((turnover / N_YEARS) * 100.0)
    
    # Matched-Static Benchmark
    w_m = mean_w / 100.0
    p_rets_static = w_m * te_ret + (1.0 - w_m) * rf_series
    cum_static = np.cumprod(1.0 + p_rets_static)
    cagr_static = (cum_static[-1] ** (1.0 / N_YEARS) - 1.0) * 100.0
    dd_static = (cum_static - np.maximum.accumulate(cum_static)) / np.maximum.accumulate(cum_static)
    max_dd_static = float(np.min(dd_static)) * 100.0
    timing_value_bps = (cagr - cagr_static) * 100.0
    
    wealth_100k = float(cum[-1] * 100000.0)
    wealth_100k_static = float(cum_static[-1] * 100000.0)
    
    return {
        "cagr": round(float(cagr), 2),
        "max_dd": round(float(max_dd), 2),
        "vol": round(float(vol), 2),
        "mean_w": round(float(mean_w), 1),
        "turnover": round(float(ann_turnover), 1),
        "cagr_static": round(float(cagr_static), 2),
        "max_dd_static": round(float(max_dd_static), 2),
        "timing_value_bps": round(float(timing_value_bps), 1),
        "wealth_100k": round(wealth_100k, 0),
        "wealth_100k_static": round(wealth_100k_static, 0)
    }

Under the Hood · Primary Engine Architecture

How Challenger C-v2 Works: Decoupled Multi-Channel Escalation

Standard frontier market crash models face a brutal dilemma: tuning for high recall causes constant false alarms, while tightening thresholds misses sudden exogenous shocks. Challenger C-v2 solves this trade-off by combining a 2-day domestic consensus gate with two decoupled exogenous fast-paths (Commodity Inflation and Global Volatility Contagion), bounded by an 8-session hold ceiling.

Pillar 01 · Domestic Consensus −25.8% False Days

2-Day Persistence Consensus Gate

Sv4[t] ≥ 0.930 && Sv4[t−1] ≥ 0.930
&& ΔS5 ≥ 0.000 && Stress_Groups ≥ 1

Domestic fragility must be verified across two consecutive market sessions with non-negative 5-day slope. This completely filters out single-session transient dips, block-trade liquidity ripples, and decelerating bull market consolidations.

Key Impact: False alert days drop from 608 to 451 sessions while maintaining 100% broad crisis recall.
Pillar 02 · Exogenous Fast-Path Immediate Bypass

Decoupled Commodity & Contagion Bypasses

Sv4 ≥ 0.90 && Stress ≥ 1 && (Ccomm ≥ 0.60 || Crisk ≥ 0.45)
Ccomm = max(Brent20d/25%, Wheat20d/25%)
Crisk = 0.40·ΔVIX5/8 + 0.20·(VIX−20)/20 + ...

Severe external shocks strike Egypt via two separate transmission channels: commodity import-bill inflation (oil/wheat drain on reserves) vs. global financial flight (VIX surge, dollar index spikes, MSCI EM selloffs). Decoupling them allows sudden crises (e.g. 2022 Ukraine, 2020 COVID) to trigger immediately at S ≥ 0.90 without waiting for a 2-day domestic lag.

Key Impact: Lifts strict early hits from 14/17 to 16/17 and advances median lead time to 17.5 sessions.
Pillar 03 · Execution Discipline Low Market Drag

Bounded Hold Duration & Cooldown

Min Hold = 5 days · Max Hold = 8 days
Refractory Cooldown = 20 days
Exit: Sv4 < 0.885 && Catalysts < 70% threshold

Caps maximum alert duration at 8 sessions (down from 12). In prolonged multi-month downturns, alerts do not drag indefinitely across consolidation recoveries. A 20-session refractory cooldown prevents redundant duplicate alerts following an episode.

Key Impact: Alert occupancy falls to 12.53%, leaving capital free to participate in 87.5% of market upside.
