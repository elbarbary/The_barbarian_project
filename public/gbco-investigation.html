00 / Start here

GB Corp in
normal English.

Egypt's biggest car group — one in five cars sold — bolted to a lending business that finances a quarter of the country's car loans. Real, large, well run, and priced against a bank deposit paying 19%.

  1. 01

    The business got better last quarter, not worse.

    Q2 revenue rose 40.9%, the gross margin widened from 15.2% to 16.9% after shrinking in Q1, and operating profit rose 48.6%. That is the opposite of what the first quarter suggested.

    +48.6% operating
  2. 02

    The profit still fell — and the cause is tax.

    Q2 pre-tax profit was flat, −0.1%. Then tax rose 127.8% and net profit fell 28.8%. The whole decline happened below the pre-tax line.

    Tax, not trading
  3. 03

    Minority shareholders are propping up the headline.

    Group profit before minorities was EGP 1,055.6m. Profit attributable to you was EGP 1,262.0m — higher, because subsidiaries' other owners absorbed EGP 206.4m of losses. The headline fall of 24.5% is really 35.5%.

    11 points kinder
  4. 04

    Half the book value is a valuation, not a factory.

    EGP 16,720.1m — exactly 50.0% of shareholders' equity — sits in one line for companies the group does not consolidate. Equity grew EGP 3.53bn in a quarter on EGP 0.83bn of profit; most of the rest was a revaluation reserve.

    50.0% of equity
  5. 05

    The bank pays 19%. This pays 7.2%.

    At EGP 31.50 the earnings yield is 7.2% and the dividend yield 1.11%, against a central-bank deposit rate of 19%. Nothing is broken here. The arithmetic of owning it at this price is the problem.

    The whole case

Real company. Wrong moment.

Liquidity The best in the series — EGP 12.5bn of float. Disclosure Also the best. Nobody else publishes this much. At EGP 31.50 Recyclable. Not a buy, not a problem.

01 / Rule five, tested

Two conditions named.
One of them fired.

On 9 August, working from Q1, this entry said the thing that would change its mind was “a quarter in which operating profit, not associates and FX, carries pre-tax profit.” Here is the Q1 picture, and here is what the 13 August release showed.

So earnings quality moved −3 → −2, and growth moved −3 → −1. In Q1 the company earned four-fifths of its pre-tax profit from an equity-accounted stake and a currency move while the actual business shrank. In Q2 it earned it by selling things, at a wider margin, with the currency working against it. That is a materially better quarter and the score has to say so.

The other condition did not fire. The entry also asked for interest cover back above 2× with net-debt/EBITDA under 1.5×. Cover improved from 1.52× to 1.60× and net-debt/EBITDA held flat at 2.14× — but auto net debt rose from EGP 13,090.9m to EGP 14,493.6m and net-debt-to-equity went 0.94× → 1.07×. The ratio improved because EBITDA grew faster than the debt, not because the debt came down. That is the weaker of the two ways to fix a balance sheet, so the pillar moves only −4 → −3.

02 / Read the line below the headline

Your profit is bigger
than the group's.

This framework has a standing instruction: look at profit attributable to owners, not the consolidated headline, when minorities are large. Usually that makes the number worse. At GB Corp it makes it better — and that is its own kind of warning.

  • H1 2025 profit before minoritiesThe whole group's earnings EGP 1,635.9m
  • H1 2026 profit before minoritiesWhat the group actually earned EGP 1,055.6m−35.5%
  • H1 2026 attributable to shareholdersAfter minorities absorbed EGP 206.4m of losses EGP 1,262.0m−24.5% — the printed figure

The gap is eleven percentage points, and it is widening. A year ago minorities contributed EGP 36.7m; this half it was EGP 206.4m, a rise of 462%. Every press summary of these results quotes the −24.5%. The group's own earnings fell by more than a third.

The same thing happened in Q1, and harder. The auto segment lost EGP 26.8m before minority interest and reported +EGP 102.9m after it. In Q2 the segment made EGP 387.9m pre-minority, so the pattern eased — but across the half the auto business earned EGP 361.2m before minorities against EGP 607.9m after.

03 / What the 1.02× book multiple is measuring

Half the book
is a valuation.

At EGP 31.50 the stock trades at about 1.02× book value, which sounds like a floor. Then you read what the book is made of.

Consolidated shareholders' equity, 31 March versus 30 June 2026
Line31 Mar 202630 Jun 2026Change
Shareholders' equity before NCIEGP 29,927.7mEGP 33,454.3m+3,526.6m

“Investments in Subsidiaries” — carried through consolidationEGP 14,088.2m (47.1% of equity)EGP 16,720.1m50.0% of equity

Book value grew EGP 3.53bn in three months on EGP 0.83bn of profit. The largest single component of the rest is a fair-value reserve that rose EGP 1,856.4m — a mark, not a cash flow. And the line holding the group's stakes in the businesses it does not consolidate — MNT-Halan, Bedaya, Kaf — now stands at exactly half of all shareholders' equity.

The company tells you this itself. GB Capital's reported return on equity carries a footnote excluding “the high equity base resulting from the revaluation effect linked to the deconsolidation of MNT-Halan.” When a business publishes a return metric and instructs you to leave part of its own equity out of the denominator, the price-to-book ratio you calculate with that equity deserves the same discount.

And there is precedent for how large these marks can be. In 4Q 2022 the group booked EGP 8,207.3m of associate income in a single quarter — on quarterly revenue of EGP 6,901.5m, a net profit margin of 126.3%. That one line was 82.2% of the year's EGP 9,985.0m of attributable profit. The following year profit fell 81.1%.

In dollars, roughly −10%. Eleven and a half years of Egyptian equity returns, almost entirely eaten by the currency. The FX rates behind that figure are approximate and are the weakest numbers in this file.

No corporate action since. A 2022 treasury cancellation trimmed the count to today's 1,085,500,000, and the ticker changed from AUTO to GBCO on 31 May 2023. A gap scan across 3,000 daily bars finds no clean-ratio break after 2016 — every large move in 2024–2026 is a real one.

Which means the run is real too. GBCO is +22.5% over a year and +386% over three, and sits 11.2% below an all-time high set on 15 July 2026. Recyclable is not a buy signal. It is the shelf you re-read when the market falls.

05 / The verdict

−6 became −2.
Same band, better quarter.

The first company in this series where nothing is broken. No regulator file, no missing head office, no 500× earnings, no unaudited accounts. KPMG signs it, the coupon gets paid, and you can sell EGP 25m of it inside a day and a half.

Valuation −2. EGP 31.50 × 1,085,500,000 = EGP 34.19bn. That is 11.87× FY2025 earnings, 13.85× trailing twelve months, 1.02× book and a 1.11% dividend yield. None of it is expensive — but the CBE overnight deposit rate is 19%, held at the 9 July meeting, and a 7.2% earnings yield against 19% risk-free cash is not a bargain. The book multiple flatters worse than the earnings multiple, for the reason set out above.

Tradability +8 — the best in the series. Free float 36.6% = 397,293,000 shares ≈ EGP 12.51bn, on median daily traded value of EGP 79.8m. At 20% of median volume, EGP 5m exits in 0.29 sessions, EGP 25m in 1.45, EGP 100m in 5.81. Held below +10 because 63.4% sits in one family trust and two strategic holders inside the “float” put the genuinely tradable portion nearer 24%.

Governance −2. The best disclosure this series has read — full segment P&L, balance sheet by segment, segment cash flow and a twelve-row ratio table every quarter, with H1 out on 13 August, three days before the press wrote it up. KPMG Hazem Hassan audits. The company answered EGX the same day on 21 January 2026 to deny an EGP 11bn syndicated-loan report. Against that: 63.4% control in one family trust; the company carried “financial leasing” in its articles for years without ever holding a licence for it, corrected in 2026 as a legal-status regularisation; and on 14 July 2026 the EGX listing committee, while approving that change, formally cautioned the company to observe Article 26 of the Listing Rules in future. The listed parent's own standalone accounts show no net profit at all in H1 2026.

What would flip it now. The interest-cover condition still stands: above 2× with net-debt/EBITDA under 1.5×, achieved by paying debt down rather than by growing into it. A second is added: a half in which attributable profit is lower than pre-minority profit — meaning the subsidiaries have stopped subsidising the headline. Or price: near EGP 20 the forward earnings yield reaches roughly 11%, and this becomes a different conversation.
