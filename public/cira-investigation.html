CIRA in
normal English.

The short answer: CIRA is a strong education business whose recent growth is real. The difficult part is the price, the nearly full campuses and one unresolved bank-contract issue.

01 / The test I expected it to fail

It grew students 19%
and cut fees in real terms.

In a country with double-digit inflation, a tuition business can grow revenue every year without teaching one extra child. That is the first thing to check, and it is where I expected this to come apart.

Higher Education is the clincher. It is 69% of revenue, and its fee per student rose 1.0% in nominal terms — a cut of roughly 10.5% in real terms — while it added 10,100 students. A company passing inflation through to families does not do that.

And the latest quarter accelerated. Q3 (March–May 2026) came in at revenue +44.5% against +30.6% at the half, normalised EBITDA +47.5%, normalised net profit +136.4%. The framework's rule that a collapsing latest quarter overrides a good three-year record points the other way here.

02 / Earnings quality

They collect the money
before they teach.

This pillar scores +5 — the best mark in the series so far, and every attack on it failed.

The four standard ways a reported profit turns out to be fake, tested against the filings
The attackVerdictEvidence
Revaluation gains inflating profitImpossibleAccounts are on a historical-cost basis (FY25 FS note 2-1-2), so the gain cannot exist
Profit is really asset disposalsNoNo disposal gains. Company-flagged non-recurring items are 4.4% of pre-tax profit
Management "adjusts" profit upwardInvertedNormalised profit is lower than statutory and reconciles to EGP 0.1m
Profit booked, cash uncollectedInvertedTrade receivables fell 3.1% to EGP 130.5m while revenue rose 35% — 10.8 days of sales, against EGP 1,179.2m of deferred revenue

Deferred revenue is 9.0× the receivable. Families pay tuition before the teaching happens, so customers fund the business rather than the other way round. That is negative working capital, and it is the single most attractive structural feature of the whole model.

It also means the "buy the buildings, then it prints money" thesis is broadly right on the economics: net debt is 1.24× EBITDA — low — and interest cover is 3.2× and rising. Seat utilisation went 64% → 67% → 88% on built seats up only 6% and faculties up 0%: they filled a building they had already paid for, and the operating leverage flowed straight through.

One serious qualification. Over the nine months, property and equipment rose EGP 96.4m, work in progress was unchanged to the pound, and investment in associates rose EGP 251.7m. The new campuses are being built inside joint ventures — Al Ahly CIRA, Cairo Egypt for Education with Elsewedy Capital, Egyptian Alliance for Education with the National Bank of Egypt. Capex appears in no filing, and CIRA's economic share of the new seats is not established. It is not buy a building and keep the profits; it is buy a share of a building with a partner.

03 / What decides it

It earns 4.69% on its price.
Its own bank account earns 18%.

This is the whole verdict in one comparison, and it needs no vendor data — both numbers come from the company's own audited notes.

  • CBE policy ratecentral bank, July 2026 19.00%

  • CIRA's earnings yieldwhat the shares earn at EGP 36.80 4.69%a ~14-point negative spread
  • Dividend yieldEGP 0.3432/share, coupon 29 0.93%

You are taking equity risk in a leveraged Egyptian mid-cap with a live covenant breach, to earn fourteen points less than the same company's treasury department earns on cash in the bank. That is the argument, and it does not depend on anyone's model of the future.

The Q4 trap — do not annualise the nine-month figure. 9M statutory EPS is EGP 2.13. Multiply it up and you get a seductive 13× — and you would be wrong. CIRA's fourth quarter runs June to August, when the academic year has ended: it books roughly 9.5 months of revenue against 12 months of cost and is structurally loss-making. Q4 adjusted net profit was −EGP 331.3m in FY23/24 and −EGP 333.5m in FY24/25. A realistic FY25/26 EPS of ~EGP 1.43–1.86 puts the forward multiple at 20–26×.

Peer context, in fairness. Against Taaleem at EGP 18.50, CIRA trades ~9% cheaper on P/E (21.3× vs 23.5×) and ~16% cheaper on EV/EBITDA (10.1× vs 12.1×), despite faster growth. Cheaper than an expensive peer is not cheap — but it means this is a judgement about the absolute price, not about CIRA being the sector's outlier.

Market cap EGP 21,446.7m across 582,790,325 shares at EGP 36.80. TTM profit attributable to owners EGP 1,005.2m, built as FY24/25 + 9M25/26 − 9M24/25. On the last audited full year alone, the multiple is 58.4×. Forward EPS is an estimate, labelled as such, and does not carry the score.

04 / Why growth is +6 and not +9

The engine is
nearly out of seats.

The growth is real. It is also, on current assets, close to finished.

  • Higher education, two years agobuilt-seat utilisation 64%
  • Last year 67%
  • Today4,943 spare seats left 88%12.1% spare
  • K-12 today2,566 spare places 93.6%6.4% spare

The +39% higher-education enrolment jump was one-time absorption of capacity that already existed. Faculties did not increase at all — still 32 — and built seats grew 6% against students up 39%. That rate cannot repeat without new campuses.

And the next campus has slipped about two years. CIRA's own credentials deck guided the first phase of ASIH Damietta to September 2025; it is now AY2027/28, with Seneca East Cairo behind it at AY2028/29. That leaves AY2026/27 as a capacity-gap year in which growth should decelerate sharply.

Half the platform is deteriorating. K-12 is 51% of students and 31% of revenue, and its segment EBITDA fell 40% in FY24/25 and a further 7% in the nine months, on revenue up 25% and 26%. The reason is regulatory rather than operational: Egypt caps private-school fee increases at 6–7% in CIRA's fee band, and has held that cap for three consecutive academic years, so same-store pricing is structurally negative in real terms.

05 / The unresolved item

What the loan note
actually says.

I published a harsher version of this section on 3 August. Then I downloaded the filing and read it. Most of what I had written did not survive.

What is confirmed, from note 14 of the Arabic 9M consolidated statements to 31 May 2026. Total borrowings including accrued interest are EGP 1,994,361,020 — the figure that had been circulating, correct to the pound. And QNB loan (2) does carry a covenant to keep total liabilities ÷ equity at or below 1.2:1, against an actual 2.63× (7,976,263,317 ÷ 3,037,865,681), improved from 4.45× a year earlier. That tension is real, and no waiver is disclosed.

What is not true. The borrowings were not all reclassified as current. Note 14 splits them EGP 1,339.8m current and EGP 654.5m non-current — and the non-current balance rose year on year, from EGP 620.4m. A third of the debt sits in non-current, which is not what a blanket reclassification looks like.

The Kuwait Finance House covenant is a different test entirely — bank loans ÷ equity ≤ 1.5, stated explicitly on a consolidated basis. On EGP 1,994.4m of bank loans against EGP 3,037.9m of equity that is 0.66×. CIRA passes it comfortably.

And there is no breach declaration. I read four consecutive pages of the loans note and found no statement that the company had breached anything, no waiver language, and no reclassification note. "Disclosed only in Arabic" was also unfair as framing: covenants live in the loans note, which is where covenants normally live.

So what is left. A leverage ratio of 2.63× against a contractual 1.2:1 in a live QNB agreement, unwaived — a real and unresolved tension. Against it: net debt of 1.24× EBITDA, interest cover of 3.2×, no default, no going-concern language, instalments current, and management still classifying EGP 654.5m as non-current, which implies it does not consider the loans callable. The pillar was revised from −2 to −1 on 4 August.

Also resolved here: the figure showing 275,357,825 shares — 47.24% of capital — as "frozen" in custody data. Note 14 discloses 8,329,190 Cairo Educational Services shares pledged, and a loan condition requiring a full pledge of the Al Ahly CIRA shares to the lender. Pledged shares appear as frozen in MCDR reporting. Ordinary bank security, disclosed in the ordinary place — not a governance finding, and it is recorded as such so it is not misused later.

07 / Two answers to the same question

The score is a rule.
The map is a decision.

An independent review ran alongside the framework, deliberately allowed to be softer. Both landed in the same place with different vocabulary — so both are published.

The independent review's price map. These are judgements, not instructions, and they move if earnings, rates, debt terms or capacity plans change.
PriceRead
Above EGP 37Do not chase. Expectations and recent momentum are both too high. This is where it trades today

The two methods agree, and the gap between them is the point. The framework says Recyclable at 36.80 and Loose Change at 20–22. The review says good company, poor fresh-entry setup, accumulate 28–31. Same conclusion about the business, same direction on price — the framework simply demands a wider margin of safety before it will say a kind word.

The asymmetry is what makes it a no today. On the review's own scenarios, a bull case gets you to roughly EGP 47 (+28%) and a bear case to roughly EGP 21 (−43%). You are being offered less upside than downside for owning a business whose growth engine is 88% full and whose bank covenants are offside.

What actually moved it in May, for completeness: on 6 May EGX approved the temporary listing of Egypt Education Platform and CIRA rose 9.9% on 8.7× median volume; on 10 May Al Borsa reported a SAR 450m Saudi expansion plan. A sector re-rating on visible, public catalysts — which is exactly the problem for a buyer arriving after them.
