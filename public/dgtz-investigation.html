01 / Rule one of the series

Run the sanitizer
before you believe a chart.

The first rule of this framework exists because Egyptian charts lie constantly — not dishonestly, just mechanically. A bonus issue manufactures a fall that never happened. So the first thing every case gets is a corporate-action test, and DGTZ passes it in the direction nobody wants.

The six tests, and what each one ruled out

Why this matters more than it sounds. In the NCCW file, a chart showing −50% over eleven years concealed a real return of +1,864%, because nine bonus distributions had quietly multiplied one share into 39.64. Here the arithmetic runs the other way: the chart shows −70% and the shareholder's loss is −70%. Nothing was manufactured, and nothing is hiding.

02 / The decisive number

Profit of EGP 16.94m.
Cash flow of minus 2.68m.

This framework has an automatic rule: if profit is recognised but most of the money is still a receivable, earnings quality scores −5 or worse. DGTZ does not fail that test narrowly. It fails it at the extreme end of the scale.

  • Unbilled contract assetsWork booked as revenue, not yet billed or paid EGP 123.89m
  • Reported net profitThe headline the market traded on EGP 16.94m
  • Cash on handWhat is standing behind all of it EGP 1.04m0.84% of the assets
  • Operating cash flowMoney the business actually generated −EGP 2.68m

The growth headline decoded. The market read +73.28% profit growth. The underlying figures are net profit of EGP 1.28m against EGP 738,700 — a rounding error dressed as growth, and published as quarterly numbers that were already eight months stale. A percentage on a base that small carries no information about the business.

And there is nothing to check it against. No audited FY2025 accounts exist. Forvis Mazars excused itself. The EGP 123.9m of contract assets has never been tested by an auditor, which is why the balance-sheet pillar scores −7 rather than something milder.

No leak signature — and I looked for one. If anyone had front-run the FRA statement, the 25–27 November pullback would show heavy volume as informed sellers left. It ran at 0.9× median volume — below normal. Nobody was quietly getting out. The market genuinely did not know until the 30th, and on the day of the inspection itself the stock closed up 3.66%. That is a finding in the market's favour, and it belongs in the file as much as anything else here.

04 / The verdict

Toxic at 2.66.
Cheap does not fix this.

Trash is a price verdict — the same company at a different price could be a different answer. Toxic is not. Every problem in this file survives a lower price, which is exactly what the band is for.

Valuation −3, and only −3. 108,000,000 shares at EGP 2.66 is a market capitalisation of EGP 287.28m. On the reported numbers that looks cheap — which is precisely the trap. It is cheap against earnings that never became cash, and this pillar is deliberately held near the middle so the score is carried by the pillars that actually decide the case.

Tradability −4 — and note the direction. Free float is 61.77% (Article 30 disclosure, period to 30 June 2026). This is not a thin-float story, which makes the collapse more meaningful, not less: a real, deep body of holders sold this down. The deduction is for the 4 December 2025 exclusion from margin trading and T+0 / T+1.

Governance −10 — the floor of the scale. The field inspection of 23 November 2025 by the FRA and the exchange, at the address the company had declared, found no premises matching the declared one; no systems and no documents could be examined, the company's representative attributing this to a move that had not been disclosed. The FRA statement of 30 November 2025 listed eight violations and refused the capital increase. An EGP 40,000 penalty followed on 6 August 2026.

What would flip it: audited FY2025 accounts showing the EGP 123.9m of contract assets converting into cash, and a resolved regulatory file. Neither exists.
