Investing 101 Episode 01

What are you paying
for one pound of profit?

That is the useful version of the P/E ratio. It is a comparison tool—not a verdict, a target price, or a reason to buy by itself.

Understand it ↓ Skip to the 18 EGX sectors
THE WRONG QUESTION Is a low P/E good? THE BETTER QUESTION Why is this company cheaper than its sector? The answer—not the ratio—is where the research starts.

01 / Before P/E

Start with the value
of the whole company.

MARKET CAP
100 million shares × EGP 10 per share

= EGP 1 billion

Market cap is what the stock market currently says all the company’s shares are worth.
↓
P/E RATIO
EGP 1bn market cap ÷ EGP 100m annual profit

= 10× earnings

At that price, you are paying EGP 10 for every EGP 1 the company currently earns in a year.

P/E = Market capitalization ÷ annual earnings. You can also calculate it as share price ÷ earnings per share. Both routes should lead to the same place when the inputs use the same earnings period and share count.

02 / Change the numbers

Calculate what the
market is asking you to pay.

YOUR RESULT
Market cap EGP 1bn
P/E ratio 10.00×

The market asks EGP 10.00 for every EGP 1 of annual profit.

03 / The comparison that matters

Compare like
with like.

COMPANY 6×
vs.
ITS SECTOR 10×

Possible interpretation: the company may be underpriced—or its earnings may be weaker, riskier or less repeatable than its peers.

  1. 1

    Use the same period. Trailing P/E should be compared with trailing P/E.

  2. 2

    Stay inside the sector. A bank and a school do not deserve the same normal multiple.

  3. 3

    Explain the gap. Check growth, debt, cash flow and whether profit came from normal operations.

  4. 4

    Then check the price. Cheap compared with peers is only useful if the earnings are real and defendable.

Do not think: low P/E = buy.

Think: why is this company cheaper than the rest of its sector?

05 / What the ratio hides

P/E tells you the price.
Not the quality.

01

Profit quality

One land sale can make a company look cheap for a year. Separate recurring operations from exceptional gains.

02

Growth direction

A 6× company with falling earnings may be more expensive than a 15× company whose earnings keep compounding.

03

Debt and cash flow

Two companies can report the same profit while one is drowning in debt and the other is generating real cash.

04

Negative earnings

If profit is zero or negative, P/E stops being meaningful. “N/A” is information—not a number to ignore.
